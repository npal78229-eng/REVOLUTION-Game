## 2. Key Cultural & Material Design Decisions

### 2.1 The Walled Mohalla & Interconnected Rooftops (*Chhats*)
- **Historical Reality:** Punjabi walled city mohallas were organic defensive enclaves organized around trade guilds (*biradaris*). Houses shared common brick walls, and flat lime-concrete rooftops formed continuous elevated paths.
- **Gameplay Translation:** Flat rooftops provide Kabir's natural traversal playground. In early story missions, Kabir uses rooftops innocently to chase fighter kites (*patang bazi*); during curfews, these identical rooftops become clandestine courier routes to bypass British street checkpoints without touching the ground.

### 2.2 The Family Workshop (Darzi / Tailor Craft)
- **Historical Reality:** Craftsmen worked on the ground-floor open veranda (*thara*) of their townhouses, directly facing the courtyard. Heavy Singer foot-pedal sewing machines, tailored wool coats for colonial clerks, and rolls of coarse khadi were standard.
- **Narrative Resonance:** Kabir's father represents ordinary artisans navigating economic strangulation—forced to repair uniform coats for the very police constables enforcing martial law, instilling in young Kabir an understanding of silent compromise vs defiance.

### 2.3 The Communal Well (*Kuan*) & Chopal
- **Historical Reality:** The communal stone well was the acoustic and social heart of Punjabi mohallas. At dawn, women collected fresh water in earthenware *gharas*; in the late afternoon, elders gathered on rope *charpais* under sacred neem/banyan trees to smoke hookahs and discuss news.
- **Gameplay Translation:** The well serves as an organic listening post. Kabir can lean against the stone plinth to overhear conversations about the Rowlatt Act, wartime price inflation, and strikes in Lahore.

### 2.4 The Grand Bazaar & Economic Extraction
- **Historical Reality:** Bazaars were the visual epicenter of colonial economic tension. Imported Lancashire machine-woven cotton was sold side-by-side with indigenous handspun *khadi*. Grain was forcibly procured by imperial agents using iron balances to supply troops overseas, causing severe local shortages.
- **Visual Storytelling:** In Zone D (The Grand Bazaar), stalls feature authentic woven cane baskets of red chili and golden turmeric, coarse burlap ration sacks with imperial crown excise stamps, and unglazed terracotta kulhad tea cups.

### 2.5 Lighting & Atmosphere: Punjab Golden Hour (3200K)
- **Historical Reality:** Late afternoon in April Punjab brings dry heat, heavy golden sunlight, loamy dust kicked up by bullock carts, and woodsmoke rising from evening cook fires (*chulhas*).
- **Engine Shading:** Set Directional Sun light to 3200K Kelvin warm illumination, 75000 lux intensity, and soft volumetric height fog scattering golden light shafts through narrow alleyways.

### 2.6 The Anglo-Vernacular Middle School (Colonial Education & Quiet Defiance)
- **Historical Reality:** Punjab Education Department schools in 1919 operated under strict colonial inspection codes. Classrooms maintained portraits of King George V, rigid timber bench rows, and regimented bell schedules. Instruction balanced basic arithmetic and English handwriting with Persian-script Urdu literacy.
- **Narrative Resonance:** In Classroom 4B, 12-year-old Kabir experiences the cognitive dissonance of reciting loyalist poetry while his classmates' older brothers distribute banned copies of the seditious newspaper *Zamindar* and handwritten notices calling for the *hartal* (general strike). The cast-iron bell marks not just the end of school, but the countdown to the evening curfew.
