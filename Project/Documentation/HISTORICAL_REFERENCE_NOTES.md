## Research pass (2026-10-12) - setting: fictional rural UP, c. 1910-1920
Sources checked (web search, standard mode):
- World Housing Encyclopedia, Housing Report 20 and related report: rural Uttar Pradesh houses are commonly unreinforced brick masonry walls in mud mortar, with flat roofs of clay tiles or bricks on beams, covered in a thick mud overlay that is renewed each year before the rains. Cement mortar is used in urban areas. This is the strongest source found for village architecture. NOTE: these are modern survey reports; the 1910s practice is inferred, not confirmed.
- Courtyard house studies (Lucknow and Varanasi): traditional courtyard houses are the common typology in UP cities. Used as general context only; it is not a rural 1910s source.
- Clothing: a 1883 Monier-Williams text describes white cotton dhoti and a second garment for respectable Hindus, and says poorer classes wore very little. A 2011 Rethinking Villages citation notes children commonly wore half-pants with a banyan or shirt. Museum records show children's angarkha tunics, but those are mainly 19th-century or Sindh/North India silk and elite pieces, not rural 1910s UP.
- Everyday objects: the flat-roof house, charpai, kulhad, matka and lantern are consistent with the existing assets and with rural North Indian life, but a period source for 1910s UP has NOT been found yet.

Confirmed-enough for design: mud-mortar brick, flat mud roofs with parapets (matches existing assets), courtyard layout, dhoti/kurta or shirt-and-shorts for boys.
Not confirmed (do not present as fact): exact 1910s UP costume details, village street layout, specific tool and cart types, school and market details for the period, and any colonial-era signage or uniforms in the village.
Next research: a photographic archive or colonial-era ethnography for UP villages (for example, a museum collection or the Census of India records of the period), and a consultant if the story needs accuracy.
